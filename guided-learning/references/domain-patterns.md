# Domain Patterns

## Mathematics

Prefer:

- prediction before calculation when useful;
- derivation and explanation of steps;
- comparison of methods;
- error analysis;
- changed-number or changed-representation variants;
- near and farther transfer after independent success.

Do not infer understanding from a final numeric answer alone.

## Programming and computer science

Prefer:

- predict program behavior before running it;
- explain state changes and invariants;
- identify bugs from evidence;
- compare implementations by behavior and trade-offs;
- modify a small example after understanding it;
- use actual execution or documentation when available for factual verification.

For learning goals, avoid completing the entire implementation before the learner has attempted the target reasoning unless the user explicitly requests direct completion.

## Natural and physical sciences

Prefer:

- predictions;
- causal explanations;
- variable manipulation;
- model comparison;
- interpretation of data and diagrams;
- boundary cases and counterexamples.

Separate observed evidence from explanatory models.

## History and social science

Prefer:

- source evaluation;
- causal chains with competing explanations;
- chronology where causally relevant;
- comparison across cases;
- evidence for and against interpretations;
- counterfactuals used cautiously to reveal causal assumptions.

Distinguish fact, interpretation, and normative judgment.

## Language learning

Prefer:

- learner production;
- contextual usage;
- correction focused on the target feature;
- retrieval and reformulation;
- discrimination between confusable forms;
- increasingly natural contexts after supported practice.

Avoid explaining grammar for too long without production.

## Fact-heavy learning

Convert passive reading into:

- prediction;
- retrieval;
- organization;
- comparison;
- explanation of relationships;
- application when the facts support a broader model.

Use direct factual teaching when derivation would be artificial.

## Professional or practical procedures

Clarify whether the target is conceptual understanding, safe execution, or both. When real-world actions have consequences, follow host safety and permission requirements and do not turn required operational warnings into a quiz.
