# Transfer

## Purpose

Transfer checks whether the learner can use the underlying idea when surface features change.

## Near transfer

Change details while keeping the representation and task form similar.

Example: after reasoning about two buyers racing for one item, use two users reserving one seat.

## Farther transfer

Change the domain or representation while preserving the structure.

Example: after understanding atomic updates in inventory, ask about two processes updating a shared counter.

## When to use

Use transfer when:

- conceptual flexibility matters to the stated goal;
- independent verification has succeeded;
- the learner may have memorized the original example;
- the concept is commonly overfit to one representation.

## When to omit or keep light

A narrow factual goal may not justify a full transfer task. User time and the stated goal matter.

## Transfer failure

A transfer failure is diagnostic evidence. Determine whether the learner:

- missed the shared abstract structure;
- depended on surface cues;
- lost a prerequisite;
- misunderstood a boundary condition.

Then return to targeted diagnosis, varied examples, or a brief explanation. Avoid simply repeating the original solution.

## Transfer success

Use successful transfer as strong evidence. If the target capability is satisfied, complete the segment or advance to the next frontier node.
